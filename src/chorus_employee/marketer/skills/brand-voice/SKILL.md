# Brand Voice

A fluent model will happily write copy that *sounds* like marketing and quietly invents numbers,
reaches for hype, or promises outcomes the product can't guarantee. This playbook is the craft that
prevents that. It is general know-how; the company's specific rules live in `brand_spec.md` — always
read that too, and when the two disagree, `brand_spec.md` wins.

## The one rule

**Never state as fact anything you cannot source.** Every performance or outcome claim is either
(a) backed by a number from research/the spec that you cite inline, or (b) hedged as a belief. There
is no third option. This single discipline is what separates on-brand from off-brand.

## Claims: substantiate or hedge

- **Substantiated** — you have a source. State it and name the source inline: "cut build time 40%
  (internal benchmark, Q2)". A figure from `web_research` is substantiated *only* if you carry its
  citation with it.
- **Hedged** — you have a hypothesis, not evidence. Frame it as one: "we believe", "early results
  suggest", "is designed to", "aims to", "can help". A hedged benefit is compliant and honest — it is
  NOT a weakness to be edited out.
- **Forbidden** — a bare metric or a guaranteed outcome stated as fact with no source: "ships 2x
  faster", "you will save 10 hours a week". Rewrite it as (a) or (b), or cut it.

## Tone

- Technical, direct, confident but grounded. Write to a smart reader who dislikes being sold to.
- Lead with the reader's problem, then the bet (what you're claiming and why), then the expected lift
  — in that order. Problem-first earns the right to make a claim.
- Prefer concrete specifics over adjectives. "Indexes a 2M-line repo in under a second" beats
  "blazingly fast".

## Prohibited patterns (rewrite on sight)

- **Hype words**: revolutionary, game-changing, 10x, unlock, supercharge, best-in-class, cutting-edge,
  seamless, effortless. (The spec may list more — treat its list as authoritative.)
- **Anti-persona**: breathless launch-hype, empty superlatives, fake urgency ("act now"), and
  "everyone is switching" bandwagon claims.
- **Wall of text**: no scannable structure. Break into a problem lead, a short section per point, and
  a clear takeaway.

## Before you finish

1. Re-read every sentence that contains a number or an outcome. Is each one sourced or hedged? If not,
   fix it now — do not leave it for the critic.
2. Scan for the prohibited words above. Replace, don't soften.
3. Check the shape: problem → bet → lift, scannable, one clear takeaway.

Do this *up front*. The brand_critic is your confirmation, not your editor — a draft that needed the
critic to catch a fabricated number was drafted wrong.
