# Channel Priors

The same message wins or dies on how it's shaped for the surface it lands on. These are defaults, not
laws — a strong reason beats a prior — but ignoring them is how good content underperforms.

## The one rule

**Fit the container to the channel, never the reverse.** Don't paste a blog post into LinkedIn or a
press release into an email. Re-shape the *format* for each surface; keep the *claim* identical.

## Per-surface priors

**Owned blog / site**
- Long-form, answer-first, discovery-driven (see the `geo-aeo-seo` skill). The durable asset.
- Success is found, read, and cited over months — not opened once. Optimize for extractability.

**Email / lifecycle**
- One idea, one call to action. Subject line does 80% of the work; preheader is the second headline.
- Respect the recipient's inbox: short, skimmable, honest. Deliverability caps volume (see the
  `deliverability` skill) — send to an engaged segment, not everyone.

**LinkedIn (B2B / leadership)**
- Point-of-view over announcement. Lead with a specific, slightly contrarian claim; short lines,
  whitespace, no link in the first line (it suppresses reach — put it in a comment).
- Where engineering/leadership benchmark tooling and process decisions.

**X / short social**
- The hook is the whole game — first line stops the scroll or nothing happens. Thread only if each
  post stands alone. Concrete beats clever.

**Landing page**
- One job: convert the intent that arrived. Comparison/positioning framing; a single primary CTA;
  proof above the fold.

## Cadence

- **Predictable rhythm beats sporadic volume** on every channel — audiences (and inbox providers)
  reward consistency.
- **Repurpose, don't duplicate.** One source asset → a distinct shape per channel (blog → LinkedIn
  POV → email → X hook), never the same text pasted everywhere.
- Match frequency to the surface: daily is fine on X, weekly on LinkedIn, monthly-ish for a
  substantive blog, and email only when you have something a segment actually wants.

## When choosing a channel (the Strategist's plan)

Ask: *where does this audience actually make the decision this campaign targets?* Pick the surface by
the buying/attention behavior, then shape the format to that surface's priors — not by what's easiest
to produce.
