# Governance and oversight

Direction drifts silently: goals block and stall, priorities go stale, work duplicates, and nobody
notices until the sprint is lost. Oversight is the executive habit of scanning the whole system,
naming every problem, and attaching a concrete corrective to each — not just observing.

## When NOT to use this
- The beat is a single narrow decision, not a whole-company review.

## The method
1. **Scan the whole tree.** Read every decision and its goals: score, priority, health, status, and
   the realizing task. Read the open proposals. Ground on the actual state, not memory.
2. **Classify each issue.** For every goal/proposal, is it: on-track, **blocked** (a landed failure),
   **drifting/stale** (no recent progress), **mis-ranked** (priority ≠ importance), **duplicated**, or
   **awaiting a decision** (a proposal)? Trace dependencies — a blocked upstream goal explains a stalled
   downstream one.
3. **Attach a corrective to each problem.** Not just "X is blocked" — say what to do: re-prioritise,
   re-scope, escalate to a human, approve/reject a proposal, or STOP it. A finding with no action is
   noise.
4. **Check the single priority is protected.** Is the org's force actually on the priority, or leaking
   into distractions? Name the leaks.
5. **Escalate what you can't safely fix.** Some issues need a human call — flag them clearly rather
   than guessing.

## Rules
- **Every issue gets an owner and an action.** Observation without a corrective is not oversight.
- **Outcomes, not vibes.** Judge health from landed verdicts, not from how busy something looks.
- **Trace dependencies.** Fix the upstream blocker before the downstream symptom.
- **Distinguish quality gates from governance gates.** Some fixes you can direct; some need sign-off.

## Common failure modes
- Listing problems with no corrective attached.
- Treating a downstream symptom instead of the upstream cause.
- Letting stale "green" goals hide because nothing re-checked them.
