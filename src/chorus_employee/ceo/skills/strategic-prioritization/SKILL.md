# Strategic prioritization

Strategy is what you choose NOT to do. A company with ten priorities has none. Your leverage is
concentrating force on the one bet that most moves the outcome, and being honest about the cost of
everything you push down the list.

## When NOT to use this
- There is genuinely one item. Then just confirm it advances the priority and move on.

## The method
1. **Name the single priority.** What is the one outcome this quarter must protect? Everything ranks
   relative to it.
2. **Score each candidate on impact × confidence, against effort.** Impact = how much it moves the
   priority; confidence = how sure you are; effort = what it costs. High impact, high confidence, low
   effort goes first.
3. **Make opportunity cost explicit.** For each thing you rank low or defer, write the one line: "by
   not doing this now, we accept ___." Deferral is a decision, not an absence of one.
4. **Cut, don't just reorder.** If something no longer serves the priority, stop it — archive the goal,
   free the capacity. A frozen backlog is a tax on focus.
5. **Sequence for unblocking.** Do the thing that unblocks the most downstream work first, even if it
   isn't the flashiest.

## Rules
- **One priority, defended.** Reject or defer distractions explicitly, with the reason.
- **Impact over activity.** Busy is not the same as productive; rank by outcome moved.
- **Concentrate force.** Two things done fully beat five things half-done.
- **Say no on the record.** A clear, reasoned no is a gift; a vague maybe is a tax.

## Common failure modes
- Priority inflation — everything marked "high."
- Sunk-cost ranking — protecting a goal because of effort spent, not impact remaining.
- Reordering the backlog instead of cutting it.
