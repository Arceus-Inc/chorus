# Stakeholder communication

A decision nobody can act on is wasted authority. Executives and boards read top-down and lose patience
with a build-up. Lead with the call; make every claim carry its evidence; be honest about the downside.
This is how the directive lands.

## When NOT to use this
- The output is an internal scratch note, not a directive anyone acts on.

## The structure (decision-first)

The call first, the reasoning second, the caveats third — the opposite of how most drafts are written.

```
DECISION (one sentence): the call you are making.
WHY (2–4 bullets): each is one reason + the specific evidence (id / number / source) that proves it.
RISKS & GUARDRAILS: the material downside of the call, each with its mitigation.
ACTIONS (ranked): the concrete next steps, highest-impact first, with an owner where you can name one.
```

If you cannot write the one-sentence decision, the thinking isn't finished — keep going, don't publish.

## Rules
- **Lead with the call.** Never bury the decision under the analysis that produced it.
- **Every claim carries evidence.** A specific id, number, or source URL — "the data supports this" is
  not evidence; "goal_2b3f is blocked (4 landed fails)" is.
- **Name the downside.** State the risk and its guardrail; false certainty destroys trust faster than a
  hedged number.
- **Rank the actions.** An unordered list of next steps hides which one matters; order by impact.
- **Self-consistency.** The summary must match the body; dates must be real (never future); a ranking
  must agree with the numbers behind it.

## Common failure modes
- Burying the decision under methodology.
- A menu of options with no recommendation (that's a report, not a directive).
- Claims with no id/number/source; risks with no guardrail.
- A summary that contradicts the body, or an impossible/future-dated claim.
