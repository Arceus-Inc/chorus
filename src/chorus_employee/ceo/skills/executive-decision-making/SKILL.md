# Executive decision-making

An organisation dies of indecision far more often than of a wrong call. Your job is not to be right
about everything — it is to make timely, reversible-where-possible calls that keep the company aimed.
This is the spine; specialist skills (prioritization, capital-allocation, risk, metrics) plug into it.

## When NOT to use this
- The beat is purely informational ("summarise the state") with no decision implied. Then just report.

## The method
1. **Frame the decision.** State the actual question in one sentence and what changes once it's made.
   If you can't write the question sharply, you're not ready to answer it.
2. **Gather the evidence you already have.** Read the company state (the decision/goal tree, health,
   proposals, outcomes) before opining. Never invent facts, ids, or numbers.
3. **Lay out the REAL options** (usually 2–3, including "do nothing"), each with its evidence and its
   opportunity cost — what you give up by choosing it.
4. **Weigh reversibility.** A reversible ("two-way door") call should be made fast and cheap; an
   irreversible ("one-way door") call earns more scrutiny and, if truly irreversible, a human gate.
5. **Commit to ONE call.** Say it in a sentence, name who owns the next action, and set a **stop
   condition** — the signal that says "this isn't working, revisit."

## Rules
- **Decide, don't hedge.** A memo that lists options without a recommendation has done half the job.
- **One priority.** Protect the company's single most important thing; subordinate everything to it.
- **Cost of delay is a cost.** Weigh the price of waiting for more certainty against acting now.
- **Own the downside.** Every material call names its worst plausible outcome and a guardrail.

## Common failure modes
- Analysis paralysis: gathering more evidence to avoid making the call.
- False precision: a confident recommendation resting on numbers you never checked.
- Deciding by consensus instead of by evidence and accountability.
