# Capital allocation

Allocating capital — dollars, headcount, attention — is the highest-leverage thing an executive does.
Returns compound where you concentrate and evaporate where you spread thin. Fund the best evidence, cut
the losers early, and never let a bet run unbounded.

## When NOT to use this
- No resources are in play (a pure information or messaging decision). Use decision-making instead.

## The method
1. **Estimate expected return per unit of resource.** For each option, roughly: (upside × probability)
   ÷ cost. Prefer the highest, adjusting for how reversible and how correlated the bets are.
2. **Fund milestones, not projects.** Release resource in tranches tied to evidence: fund the next
   milestone, not the whole plan. Re-decide at each gate.
3. **Set a stop-loss up front.** Before funding, write the condition under which you STOP — the metric,
   the date, the burn. A bet with no kill-criterion is a runaway.
4. **Rebalance toward what's working.** Move resource from proven-weak to proven-strong on evidence,
   not on who asks loudest.
5. **Protect a reserve.** Don't commit 100%; keep slack for the opportunity or fire you can't foresee.

## Rules
- **Concentrate on the best evidence,** don't peanut-butter resources across everything.
- **Every bet has a stop-loss** — a metric and a date that trigger a cut.
- **Reversible first.** Prefer staged, reversible commitments over one big irreversible one.
- **Sunk cost is sunk.** Decide the next dollar on future return, not past spend.

## Common failure modes
- Funding the whole plan up front, then being unable to stop.
- No kill-criteria — losing bets that run because nobody set a stop.
- Over-diversifying until nothing gets enough force to work.
